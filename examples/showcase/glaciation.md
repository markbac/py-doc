## Introduction {.abstract}

Twenty thousand years ago an ice sheet a kilometre thick covered Scotland. Glaciers flowed from it down the valleys, plucking and grinding the rock beneath. When the ice melted it left the landscape we walk today: corries, arêtes, U-shaped valleys, moraines and drumlins.

## 1. The glacier budget: gain and loss

```balance
left: Snow | 40 | brand
left: Avalanches | 8 | second
right: Melt | 36 | spark
right: Calving | 9 | blue
right: Vapour | 2 | grey
```

In millions of cubic metres a year, for a valley glacier. A glacier advances when accumulation is more than ablation, and retreats when it is less. In balance, the snout stays still, but the ice still flows.

## 2. Inside the ice: why it flows

```cutaway type=column unit=" m" direction=down
0 | 50 | Brittle ice | Crevasses open as it flows | blue
50 | 280 | Plastic ice | Deforms and flows downhill | second
280 | 300 | Basal ice | Full of debris that grinds the bed | brand
```

Under its own weight, ice deeper than about 50 m stops cracking and flows like very thick toffee, fastest at the surface and the centre.

## 4. Erosion: how ice shapes rock

```cards cols=2 style=label
Plucking | Meltwater freezes onto jointed rock and pulls blocks away as the ice moves
Abrasion | Rock in the base of the ice scratches and polishes the bed, leaving striations
Freeze-thaw | Water in cracks freezes, swells by 9 % and splits the rock above the ice
Rotational slip | Ice in a corrie turns as it slides, deepening the hollow
```

```steps
Snow gathers | In a north-facing hollow, out of the sun
Firn to ice | Compressed over decades into glacier ice
Plucking and abrasion | Steepen the back wall and deepen the floor
A corrie | When the ice melts, often holding a tarn | brand
```

## 8. Today: the ice in retreat

```highlight
3.2 km | Aletsch Glacier, shorter since 1880 | The Alps' largest | blue
−0.8 m | Of ice thickness lost a year | Average of reference glaciers | brand
```

Scotland's last snow patches, in the corries of Ben Nevis and Braeriach, now melt out most summers: a reminder of how little colder it would need to be for the corries to fill again.

## In short {.summary}

The ice is gone, but every Highland glen is a record of how it moved.

### Erosion

Plucking and abrasion carve corries, arêtes and U-shaped valleys.

### Deposition

Melting ice drops moraines, drumlins and erratics.

### The budget

Gain against loss decides whether a glacier grows or shrinks.
