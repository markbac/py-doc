## Introduction {.abstract}

DNA stores the instructions; proteins do the work. A cell copies a gene into messenger RNA, carries it out of the nucleus, and a ribosome reads it three bases at a time, joining one amino acid for each codon. This poster follows one short gene from DNA to protein.

## 2. The steps: gene to protein

```flow
[DNA|brand] -transcription-> [mRNA|second] -leaves the nucleus-> [Ribosome|grey] -translation-> [Protein|brand]
```

```steps
Unzip | RNA polymerase opens the DNA at the gene's promoter
Transcribe | It builds mRNA, base by base, from the template strand | second
Splice | Introns are cut out; the exons are joined
Export | The mRNA leaves the nucleus through a pore
Translate | A ribosome reads it codon by codon | brand
Fold | The chain folds into a working protein | brand
```

## 3. Words: a glossary

```glossary cols=2

Exon | A part of a gene kept in the mRNA
Intron | A part cut out before translation
Promoter | Where RNA polymerase starts to read
Mutation | A change in the DNA sequence
```

## 4. The code: three bases, one amino acid

```tape
cells: A U G G C U A A A U G G U A A
1-3 | Met (start) | green
4-6 | Ala | second
7-9 | Lys | brand
10-12 | Trp | second
13-15 | Stop | red
```

Read in threes from AUG, this mRNA codes for Met–Ala–Lys–Trp and then stops. A single base added or lost shifts every codon after it: a frameshift.

## 6. Where the genome goes {.wide}

```chart type=bar title="The human genome, by what it does" unit=" %" height=230
Repeats: transposons and copies | 45
Introns: cut out of the mRNA | 25
Not yet understood | 20.5
Regulation: promoters, enhancers | 8
Protein-coding: the genes on this poster | 1.5
```

Only one and a half per cent of our DNA spells out proteins. The rest switches genes on and off, is cut away, or is copies of old viruses and jumping genes.

## In short {.summary}

One direction of flow: DNA makes RNA makes protein.

### Transcription

In the nucleus, DNA is copied into mRNA.

### Translation

At the ribosome, each codon adds one amino acid.

### Mutation

One wrong base can change, or stop, the protein.
